# Erratum: what #285 changes in the late-local errata

Date 2026-11-24. Raised by WP-LO1's PR for #285, which fixes four of CodeRabbit's comments on #263 that the review rounds didn't cover, and what its review found (#309). docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md and docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-follow-ups.md are oracles, so this file records where the PR makes them out of date. Status: decided 2026-11-24 by the orchestrator for Karim, as #285's follow-ups to D-late-local.

## Input

- docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-follow-ups.md, What the code does, on the mark a sweep's base frame keeps: "It makes the mark due again while no batch has been sealed with the records from it on, but only from the next record on."
- docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md, What the code does, Stretches: "When its edges share no monotonic clock, as for a stretch left open across a reboot, one that began at a moment Open read back from a row, or one rebuilt from an older version's rows, nothing tells how far the wall clock stepped, so the stretch holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the stretch before it, or from the beginning of time for the first, as #174's single moment held every record before it."
- docs/DESIGN.md 3.6, A batch's mode: "A stretch whose edges share no monotonic clock, as one left open across a reboot, holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the stretch before it."
- docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md, Still open: "As #174's Still open says of a step of the wall clock where an event and the moment share no monotonic clock: a stretch whose edges share none holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the stretch before it, so a step back longer than the time between the two stretches can still date such a record before every stretch it was made in, and two steps inside one run that cancel out leave its edges' spans equal, so the stretch isn't widened. Such a record seals online."
- #285, items 1 to 3: a due mark lost when a local-only batch seals before the mark is taken and the agent restarts; a learned stretch whose wall bounds come out inverted; and whether a sweep should write `batches.seg` again for live state that no seal frame has recorded yet.

## Expected

The mark due for the first batch after a switch back outlasts a restart, for a batch whose records were all made after it, until a batch sealed online takes it, as it outlasts one with no batch sealed since by the agent's Seen. A stretch whose wall bounds the clocks' order leaves inverted holds what its end's boot made in it before its end, whatever that boot's wall clock read. A sweep writes `batches.seg` again for retention only.

## Computed

On main at 3c9268f4:

- The writer keeps the mark only in memory. It drops the mark when a batch sealed `Back` takes it, and a base frame keeps it across a sweep. After a switch back, a held call can make the first batch local-only, which doesn't take the mark. If the agent then restarts, nothing restores the mark. The base frame's mark counts only while no batch covers its records, and the agent's `Seen` arms one only when no batch was sealed after the end. The first batch sealed online then has no mark, and the uploader keeps it in the local-only run. The cost is that batch's credit.
- When `clockOrder` orders a learned stretch's edges by the run's or a boot's start, its wall bounds can come out inverted. That happens when the wall clock reads behind after a reboot, and `widen` takes the start's wall time from the stretch before it in the list, whose wall end is later. A record with no monotonic reading that the wall clock dates before the end then falls outside every stretch, though it may have been made in this one, and it seals online. A stretch that went on across such a reboot, which `sawOnline` ends in the new boot, comes out the same way, as does the one going on that Open ends at an older version's row, as after a downgrade, by a boot's clock under which the wall clock reads behind.

## What the code does with #285

- **Every seal frame records the mark** the writer has due once its batch is sealed, 0 for none, as when the batch took or ended it (`sealPayload.Mark`). The base frame a sweep writes records the writer's mark then, with the newest batch sealed then (`basePayload.Mark`, `MarkAt`), as #359 made them. Open makes due again the mark that the newest of these frames records: the base frame's while no row of a batch sealed after the sweep is kept, since it is newer than every row the sweep kept, and the newest row's otherwise. Frames an older version wrote record the mark only by a seal frame's `Due`, when its batch didn't take it, a row's `back_ended`, and the base frame's `Back`. For those, Open makes the mark due again from the newest row. With no row kept, or when the newest row records none and its records end before the mark the base frame keeps, as a row an older version sealed does, the base frame's mark is due, and a newest row that ended the mark Open restored leaves none. With no row kept that is so whenever the base frame records one, where #263 also required that no batch before the sweep held its records, which lost the mark when the swept batch was the local-only one sealed for a call on its way. When the newest row records none and ends before the mark, it is #263's rule. The mark is due again only from the next record on, never for a record a crash left pending, as #263's last round made it, and also when a power cut took records numbered before it, whose numbers the next records take again.
- **A mark Open restores marks only a batch whose records were all made after Open** (`Writer.backFrom`), by their own times and by their senders' stamps, as Write holds them to the stretches, and never one that holds a git change, which goes by the scan that found it, whenever its commit was made. A monotonic reading of another boot or another run counts as made before Open whatever the wall clock says, since Open runs in this boot and this run. After its newest seal frame, the running writer may have cleared the mark without sealing a batch, as at a seal's read that finds `config.toml` local-only with nothing pending, and begun a stretch that no frame recorded. A restart erases that stretch with the mark it cleared. A record of it can still reach the writer after Open, as a hook call held.jsonl kept or a transcript line read late, and no stretch the writer knows holds it. But it was made before Open, since a stretch still going on at Open clears the mark at Open's own read. So a batch that holds such a record stays unmarked and closes the local-only run with itself inside, as on main. Any batch sealed online that doesn't take the mark ends it: one that holds such a record, one that holds a git change however new its commit, and Open's own batch of what a crash left, whose first record comes before the mark. The mark ends once that batch's frame is written, the frame records none due, and no later seal asks Waiting for it.
- **A stretch whose wall bounds the clocks' order leaves inverted**, whether learned, ended by `sawOnline`, or ended by Open at an older version's row, holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the newest other stretch that the wall clock puts at or before its end, or from the beginning of time (`orderWall`, after each `widen`). The same rule orders the stretches Open rebuilds from an older version's rows, and the stretch a seal's read ends for its question to Waiting. A stretch whose edges share no monotonic clock already holds what the wall clock dates from the end of the stretch before it in the list (`widen`). This takes the stretch the wall clock puts before it instead. That closes the part of #174's known limit for the records the wall clock dates before such a stretch's end, as its end's boot made them, when the clocks order the edges but that boot's wall clock reads behind. Once a batch has been sealed online after the stretch's end, it costs credit only. Before that, a record the added range holds, online ones and those of a passed stretch it overlaps included, makes a local-only batch, which after a `config.toml` edit back online stops the roots, as the late-local erratum's second Still open item says.
- **A sweep doesn't write `batches.seg` again for live state.** It writes the file again only to drop rows, fold stretches or fold moments. A stretch end the writer took after its newest seal stays for the next seal frame to record. A restart before that seal reads the stretch as going on, from the frame that recorded it, until the agent's Seen gives the end it kept, which ends the stretch there and makes the mark due again. Without that Seen, the first read that finds `config.toml` online ends it, later than it ended, and marks no batch, so the first online batch after the restart stays in the local-only run. Both cost credit only, as the late-local erratum's first Still open item says. Rewriting `batches.seg` for this would cost a rewrite at most starts, for what the first seal records anyway.

## Still open

- The rest of #174's known limit: a step back of the wall clock longer than the time between two stretches, and two steps that cancel out inside one run, when the edges share no monotonic clock.
- A stretch whose wall bounds came out inverted still holds no record its start's boot made in it with no monotonic reading an edge holds, as a transcript line read late or a held hook call of that boot, that the boot's wall clock dates after the stretch's end. Such a record seals online, as before #285: nothing bounds that boot's records by the wall clock.
- A record of a stretch a restart erased that carries no monotonic reading, a log line its reader dates at the read because the wall clock reads behind the line's own time, or a log line its reader dates from the read by the wall clock's gap, as one read within LATE_READ_S of its time, that a step back of the wall clock dates after Open, counts as made after Open, so its batch can take the mark Open restored. This is #174's known limit for a step of the wall clock, applied to the restored mark.

## Decision

- Every seal frame records the mark the writer has due once its batch is sealed, 0 for none (`sealPayload.Mark`), and the base frame records the writer's mark at the sweep, with the newest batch sealed then (`basePayload.Mark`, `MarkAt`), as #359 made them. Open makes due again the mark the newest of these frames records: the base frame's while no row of a batch sealed after the sweep is kept, and the newest row's otherwise. In frames an older version wrote, which record the mark only by a seal frame's `Due`, a row's `back_ended` and the base frame's `Back`, Open makes it due again from the newest row, or from the base frame when no row is kept, or when the newest row records none and its records end before that mark, and leaves none due when the newest row ended the mark Open restored. It is due only from the next record on. A mark Open restores then marks only a batch whose records were all made after Open, by their own times and their senders' stamps, a monotonic reading of another boot or another run counting as made before Open, and none that holds a git change, which goes by the scan that found it. A batch that holds an older record stays unmarked and closes the local-only run with itself inside. Any batch sealed online that doesn't take the mark ends it, once its frame is written, and the frame records none due: one that holds an older record, one that holds a git change however new its commit, and Open's own batch of what a crash left. This replaces the follow-ups erratum's sentence quoted above.
- A stretch can come out with its wall bounds inverted when the clocks' order leaves them so, whether it was learned, ended by `sawOnline`, or ended by Open's downgrade. For such a stretch, the start's wall reading is the newest end of another stretch that the wall clock puts at or before the stretch's end, or the beginning of time. The same holds for a stretch Open rebuilds from an older version's rows, and for the one a seal's read ends to ask Waiting. That replaces the late-local erratum's "the end of the stretch before it". That erratum's Still open item on a step of the wall clock stays, except for the records that the end's boot dates between that end and the stretch's own end.
- #285 item 3: a sweep writes batches.seg again only to drop rows, fold stretches or fold moments. The late-local erratum's first Still open item stays, and it covers the mark as well as the stretch's end.
- DESIGN 3.6 and 4.2 are unchanged. For an inverted stretch, 3.6's "the end of the stretch before it" is read by the wall clock. docs/spec/ledger-v1.md is unchanged, apart from #305's sentence on a call timed by the agent's own stamp.
