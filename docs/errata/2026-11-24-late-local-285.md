- docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md, Still open: "As #174's Still open says of a step of the wall clock where an event and the moment share no monotonic clock: a stretch whose edges share none holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the stretch before it, so a step back longer than the time between the two stretches can still date such a record before every stretch it was made in, and two steps inside one run that cancel out leave its edges' spans equal, so the stretch isn't widened. Such a record seals online."
- #285, items 1 to 3: a due mark lost when a local-only batch seals before the mark is taken and the agent restarts; a learned stretch whose wall bounds come out inverted; and whether a sweep should write `batches.seg` again for live state that no seal frame has recorded yet.

## Expected

The mark due for the first batch after a switch back outlasts a restart until a batch sealed online takes it, as it outlasts one with no batch sealed since. A record a learned stretch may hold is held, whatever the wall clock read in the stretch's end's boot. A sweep writes `batches.seg` again for retention only.

## Computed

On main at 3c9268f4:

- The writer keeps the mark only in memory. It drops the mark when a batch sealed `Back` takes it, and a base frame keeps it across a sweep. After a switch back, a held call can make the first batch local-only, which doesn't take the mark. If the agent then restarts, nothing restores the mark. The base frame's mark counts only while no batch covers its records, and the agent's `Seen` arms one only when no batch was sealed after the end. The first batch sealed online then has no mark, and the uploader keeps it in the local-only run. The cost is that batch's credit.
- When `clockOrder` orders a learned stretch's edges by the run's or a boot's start, its wall bounds can come out inverted. That happens when the wall clock reads behind after a reboot, and `widen` takes the start's wall time from the stretch before it in the list, whose wall end is later. A record with no monotonic reading that the wall clock dates before the end then falls outside every stretch, though it may have been made in this one, and it seals online.

## What the code does with #285

- **Every seal frame records the mark** the writer had due when its batch didn't take it (`sealPayload.Due`). Open makes the mark due again from the newest row. With no row kept, or when the newest row records none and its records end before the mark a sweep's base frame keeps (`basePayload.Back`), as a row an older version sealed does, the base frame's mark is due, as #263 had it. The mark is due again only from the next record on, never for a record a crash left pending, as #263's last round made it.
- **A learned stretch whose wall bounds the clocks' order leaves inverted** holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the newest stretch the wall clock puts before its end, or from the beginning of time. A stretch whose edges share no monotonic clock already holds what the wall clock dates from the end of the stretch before it in the list (`widen`). This takes the stretch the wall clock puts before it instead. That closes the part of #174's known limit where the clocks order a learned stretch's edges but the wall clock reads behind in its end's boot. It costs credit only.
- **A sweep doesn't write `batches.seg` again for live state.** It writes the file again only to drop rows, fold stretches or fold moments. A stretch end the writer took after its newest seal stays for the next seal frame to record. A restart before that seal reads the stretch as going on, from the frame that recorded it. The first read that finds `config.toml` online then ends it, later than it ended, so only more counts as local-only. Rewriting `batches.seg` for this would cost a rewrite at most starts, for what the first seal records anyway.
