## Decided

Decided 2026-11-20 by the orchestrator for Karim. Neither option above stands as written.

A permission answer counts only when the harness itself reports who gave it (SM 1.2). For Claude Code that is its `claude_code.tool_decision` for the call, from a connection the peer-process check traces to the process whose hooks reported the request (DESIGN 5.2). Its `source` gives `decided_by`: `user_temporary`, `user_permanent`, `user_reject` and `user_abort` are the user, and `hook` and `config` are those. A request with no such report writes no record. Pairing `PermissionRequest` with `PostToolUse` alone can't say who answered, since a `PermissionRequest` hook can answer for him.

`PermissionRequest` gives `requested_at`, and `answered_at` is the earliest of the report's `event.timestamp`, its arrival and the call's `PostToolUse`. An accepted edit applies the moment it is accepted, so SM's five seconds between the accept and the edit were the slip: SM 6.1's first edit moves to 14:05:25, when its `PostToolUse` arrives, and every number stays (14:05 is `SUP` 10, `PA` 25, `SUP` 25, and the bucket holds 303⅓ s at 14:09:00). The record is written when the report arrives, with `t` = `answered_at` and the hook's `source` and `via`, so it is a hook anchor, level A in 6.1.

Two statements above are wrong. An `otel` anchor is level C, not A: DESIGN 6.4 names only `hook`, `log`, `shell`, `git` and `editor` for levels A and B, and so does `pkg/score`'s `levelVia`. And the `tool.blocked_on_user` span is documented (PA 1.11), but only with beta tracing, which DESIGN 5.1 doesn't turn on. The arithmetic of the hooks-only reading is right.

Item 2: yes. WP-AD1's capture checks the event's `source` and timestamp, a hook's answer and which process sends the export before WP-AD4 relies on them, and without a `source` Claude Code permission answers open nothing.
