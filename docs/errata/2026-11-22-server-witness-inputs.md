# Erratum: DESIGN 7.4's offline label rests on upload-failure notes that DESIGN 6.5's reveal set never opens, its lookback rule on a mark no record holds, and SP 8.4's clock correction on readings ingest doesn't send

Date 2026-11-22. Raised by WP-SV6, which builds `internal/server/witness`. Status: decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim, in issue #211. Decisions 1 and 3 amended the same day in #203's second round, from the fix-verify in #211. Applied to DESIGN 6.5 and 7.4 and to PLAN's WP-SB3 and WP-SB4 by #203, the PR that raised it, under the oracle-change label.

## Input

- DESIGN 7.4's table: `offline` is "Late, but sealed on a continuous chain while the ledger holds upload-failure notes, then flushed in one catch-up", and `backfilled` is "Everything else late, and all lookback".
- DESIGN 3.6: the notes are records of rtype `0x41`, and "They support the `offline` label (7.4) and are the client's own claim". ledger-v1 4.7 gives a note's body as `{"t": t, "reason": r}`, and 4.3 gives it no project and the session `none`.
- DESIGN 6.5's reveal set opens the bodies of (a) records whose header project is in `P`, (b) every `os.presence`, `os.focus` and `os.power` record, (c) every `editor.activity` record, (d) the records of sessions that share a terminal with `P`, (e) `P`'s checkpoints and (f) every record of every project where focus or presence is unknown. "Every other record in an overlapping batch: header and body hash only." A note has no project, so no rule opens it.
- A lookback record's body is an event like any other (ledger-v1 4.5). The agent's writer tells lookback's records apart by `ledger.Input.Lookback` and by the chain's creation time (`internal/agent/ledger`, `Writer.Created`), and neither reaches a body or the server.
- DESIGN 7.4: "Event times are re-anchored from the monotonic clock within one boot". SP 8.4 corrects a device's clock by `server_received_ms - clock.wall_ms`, from a wall and a monotonic reading every batch carried at send time. ingest-v1's batch roots carry no clock (2.3.1), and `X-Pairtime-Time` is the client's clock already corrected by the offset it measured (2.1).

## Decision

1. **Reveal rule (g)**, as amended in #203's second round. DESIGN 6.5's reveal set gains "(g) every upload-failure note in the batches that overlap the range, and, for each catch-up a revealed batch belongs to, the `os.presence`, `os.focus` and `os.power` bodies of the batch before it, and, while that batch itself came in a catch-up, of the batch before that catch-up too, and so on. A note's body holds only a time and a reason code, and those presence, focus and power bodies are the kinds (b) already opens." So the anchors cost no new kind of data. WP-SB3's Build and CI checks gain the same rule. The first text opened only the batch before each catch-up. A backlog that reached the server in two catch-ups then read `backfilled` from what it reveals, since the batch before the second catch-up came late itself and could read `offline` only through notes near its own close, which the range needn't overlap. A catch-up's anchor that came in an earlier catch-up now holds when that catch-up's own anchor holds, by the run rules a catch-up's records already use: its run resumed after a silence, and it reached the server within `WITNESS_LAG_S` of the run's first batch. Such an anchor needs no note of its own.
2. **Lookback.** WP-SB3's metadata reveal carries the chain's creation time. The server marks a record as lookback's by the agent's own rule (`internal/agent/ledger`'s `Writer.lookback`: via `log`, dated before the chain began, carrying no reading of the run's clock) and passes the mark as `witness.Record.Lookback`.
3. **Re-anchoring**, as amended in #203's second round. DESIGN 7.4 says what re-anchoring corrects: against the server's receipts, and never to a time after the record's own wall time, where its credit lands, so a device clock that runs slow reads late, and one more than 5 minutes fast has its records refused. The client's own reading follows the same rule, so a teen's dashboard never reads witnessed or offline a minute whose records a submission refuses: the uploader reads each minute by the device's clock, with no correction for the offset it measured, and reads it backfilled when, for any batch from the first one sealed after the minute's start through the first one sealed after its end, the earlier of the minute's end and that batch's seal is more than 5 minutes after that batch reached the server. A refused minute still reads backfilled, so the dashboard shows level-C credit that a submission refuses. `pairtime doctor` warns when the measured offset is more than a few minutes either way, and says that live work reads late until the clock is set right. Amended in #203's second round: the first text read the minute by the first batch sealed after its end alone, while a submission checks each record against its own batch's receipt.
4. **Header positions.** Timing a note by its neighbours in the revealed headers was considered and not taken. DESIGN 6.5 reveals every header of an overlapping batch, a note's sequence number places it between the records written before and after it, and rule (b)'s presence bodies would bound its time to within about a batch, though only from records sampled live, since a record written late carries an earlier time. Rule (g) gives the note's own time instead, at the cost of a time and a reason code per note.

## What the code does

1. A note supports `offline` only when its body was revealed: the failure has to fall within `WITNESS_LAG_S` of the late record's batch closing, before or after it, in a batch of the same catch-up or a later one. The client's own reading counts each failure together with the wait before its next try (`internal/agent/uploader`, `waitedOut`), which the server can't see. A note before the close stands in for that wait, which the uploader caps at 10 minutes without a `Retry-After`.
2. A late record reads `offline` only when the batch before its catch-up, its anchor, holds: the anchor has a timed record that reads `witnessed` or `offline`, or it came itself in an earlier catch-up whose anchor holds, by the run rules above. Rule (g)'s presence, focus and power bodies supply each anchor's records, so a chain of anchors always goes back to a batch with a record that reads `witnessed`. A batch closes no earlier than any batch before it, so every batch of the catch-up closed no earlier than the time of that witnessed record, and that time is at most `WITNESS_LAG_S` before its batch reached the server. That holds whatever records a caller leaves out. Without it, once notes are revealed, a batch of backdated records and a note, sent after the device got batches of the present through, would read `offline` for any past window it claims, and leaving the records of the batch before out of the reveal would hide it. Which records inside the bound read `offline` can still depend on what a caller passes: a record no rule opens, dated late in a batch of the first of two catch-ups, raises the close of every batch after it, so leaving it out lets records of the second catch-up read `offline` that read `backfilled` with it.
3. `witness.Record.Lookback` is the caller's, and WP-SB4 sets it by the agent's rule above. A record so marked reads `backfilled` whatever its lag, so the mark can only take credit away.
4. The corrected time is the earliest of the record's wall time, its batch's `received_at`, and the `received_at` of every record of its boot whose monotonic clock read later, less the monotonic time between them. A record's credit lands at its wall time, which the client scored and a submission re-scores, so a correction to a later time would let a backdated record read fresh. A wall clock that runs slow reads late by as much as it is slow: a device clock 20 minutes slow gets `backfilled` for live work. One that runs fast by up to 5 minutes reads by the server's clock. One that runs faster has every record it sends live refused at submission. SP 8.4 corrected first and refused after, from a send-time offset ingest doesn't have.
5. The uploader's reading (`witnessOf`) follows the same rule, so a device clock 20 minutes slow reads its live minutes `backfilled`, at its own end and at ingest. A minute that may hold a record dated more than 5 minutes after its own batch reached the server reads `backfilled` too: for each batch from the first one sealed after the minute's start through the first one sealed after its end, the uploader compares the earlier of the minute's end and the batch's seal with the batch's receipt (`refused`). So a clock 6 minutes fast that seals at :50, each root received 15 s after its seal, reads its minutes `backfilled`, though the batch after each minute reached the server less than 5 minutes before the minute's end, and a clock less than 5 minutes fast never reads `backfilled` by the check. Ingest's label never betters the reading. The uploader tells doctor, as `problem.uploader`, when the offset it measured is more than 2 minutes either way.
