# Erratum: DESIGN 7.4's offline label rests on upload-failure notes that DESIGN 6.5's reveal set never opens, its lookback rule on a mark no record holds, and SP 8.4's clock correction on readings ingest doesn't send

Date 2026-11-22. Raised by WP-SV6, which builds `internal/server/witness`. Status: decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim, in issue #211. Applied to DESIGN 6.5 and 7.4 and to PLAN's WP-SB3 and WP-SB4 by #203, the PR that raised it, under the oracle-change label.

## Input

- DESIGN 7.4's table: `offline` is "Late, but sealed on a continuous chain while the ledger holds upload-failure notes, then flushed in one catch-up", and `backfilled` is "Everything else late, and all lookback".
- DESIGN 3.6: the notes are records of rtype `0x41`, and "They support the `offline` label (7.4) and are the client's own claim". ledger-v1 4.7 gives a note's body as `{"t": t, "reason": r}`, and 4.3 gives it no project and the session `none`.
- DESIGN 6.5's reveal set opens the bodies of (a) records whose header project is in `P`, (b) every `os.presence`, `os.focus` and `os.power` record, (c) every `editor.activity` record, (d) the records of sessions that share a terminal with `P`, (e) `P`'s checkpoints and (f) every record of every project where focus or presence is unknown. "Every other record in an overlapping batch: header and body hash only." A note has no project, so no rule opens it.
- A lookback record's body is an event like any other (ledger-v1 4.5). The agent's writer tells lookback's records apart by `ledger.Input.Lookback` and by the chain's creation time (`internal/agent/ledger`, `Writer.Created`), and neither reaches a body or the server.
- DESIGN 7.4: "Event times are re-anchored from the monotonic clock within one boot". SP 8.4 corrects a device's clock by `server_received_ms - clock.wall_ms`, from a wall and a monotonic reading every batch carried at send time. ingest-v1's batch roots carry no clock (2.3.1), and `X-Pairtime-Time` is the client's clock already corrected by the offset it measured (2.1).

## Decision

1. **Reveal rule (g).** DESIGN 6.5's reveal set gains "(g) every upload-failure note in the batches that overlap the range, and, for each catch-up a revealed batch belongs to, the `os.presence`, `os.focus` and `os.power` bodies of the batch before it". A note's body holds only a time and a reason code. The extra presence, focus and power bodies are the kinds rule (b) already opens, so the anchor costs no new kind of data. WP-SB3's Build and CI checks gain the same rule.
2. **Lookback.** WP-SB3's metadata reveal carries the chain's creation time. The server marks a record as lookback's by the agent's own rule (`internal/agent/ledger`'s `Writer.lookback`: via `log`, dated before the chain began, carrying no reading of the run's clock) and passes the mark as `witness.Record.Lookback`.
3. **Re-anchoring.** DESIGN 7.4 says what re-anchoring corrects: against the server's receipts, and never to a time after the record's own wall time, where its credit lands, so a device clock that runs slow reads late, and one more than 5 minutes fast has its records refused. The client's own reading follows the same rule, so a teen's dashboard never promises what a submission refuses: the uploader reads each minute by the device's clock, with no correction for the offset it measured, and reads it backfilled when it ends more than 5 minutes after its batch reached the server. `pairtime doctor` warns when the measured offset is more than a few minutes either way, and says that live work reads late until the clock is set right.
4. **Header positions.** Timing a note by its neighbours in the revealed headers was considered and not taken. DESIGN 6.5 reveals every header of an overlapping batch, a note's sequence number places it between the records written before and after it, and rule (b)'s presence bodies would bound its time to within about a batch, though only from records sampled live, since a record written late carries an earlier time. Rule (g) gives the note's own time instead, at the cost of a time and a reason code per note.

## What the code does

1. A note supports `offline` only when its body was revealed: the failure has to fall within `WITNESS_LAG_S` of the late record's batch closing, before or after it, in a batch of the same catch-up or a later one. The client's own reading counts each failure together with the wait before its next try (`internal/agent/uploader`, `waitedOut`), which the server can't see. A note before the close stands in for that wait, which the uploader caps at 10 minutes without a `Retry-After`.
2. A late record reads `offline` only when the batch before its catch-up has a timed record that reads `witnessed` or `offline`, which rule (g)'s presence, focus and power bodies supply. That batch closed in time, so every batch of the catch-up closed no earlier than `WITNESS_LAG_S` before that batch reached the server. Without it, once notes are revealed, a batch of backdated records and a note, sent after the device got batches of the present through, would read `offline` for any past window it claims, and leaving the records of the batch before out of the reveal would hide it.
3. `witness.Record.Lookback` is the caller's, and WP-SB4 sets it by the agent's rule above. A record so marked reads `backfilled` whatever its lag, so the mark can only take credit away.
4. The corrected time is the earliest of the record's wall time, its batch's `received_at`, and the `received_at` of every record of its boot whose monotonic clock read later, less the monotonic time between them. A record's credit lands at its wall time, which the client scored and a submission re-scores, so a correction to a later time would let a backdated record read fresh. A wall clock that runs slow reads late by as much as it is slow: a device clock 20 minutes slow gets `backfilled` for live work. One that runs fast by up to 5 minutes reads by the server's clock. One that runs faster has every record it sends live refused at submission. SP 8.4 corrected first and refused after, from a send-time offset ingest doesn't have.
5. The uploader's reading (`witnessOf`) follows the same rule, so a device clock 20 minutes slow reads its live minutes `backfilled`, at its own end and at ingest. One more than 5 minutes fast reads its minutes `backfilled` too: the uploader holds such a clock's buckets back until their minute starts within 240 s of the server's clock, and they arrive read `backfilled`, which ingest's label never betters. The uploader tells doctor, as `problem.uploader`, when the offset it measured is more than 2 minutes either way.
