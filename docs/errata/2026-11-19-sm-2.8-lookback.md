# Erratum: what SM 2.8 leaves open for lookback

Date 2026-11-19. Raised by WP-FX5. Status: waiting for Karim. Until Karim decides, `testdata/timelines/lookback` follows the readings below, and every row whose numbers depend on one of them names this erratum.

SM 2.8 is five sentences long. Applied by hand to 6.1, 6.4 and a two-session case, it decides most of what those timelines credit. Points 2 to 5 are where it doesn't. Point 1 is resolved, and it keeps its number so the others keep theirs. The list at the end covers what the fixtures don't reach.

## 1. Resolved: each of 6.1's turns reads 120 s

This point quoted the in-progress docs/PLAN.md, whose WP-FX5 said 6.1's twelve-minute cycles credit no reading. The plan as merged (68726d8) says the stop at 14:06:30 and the next prompt at 14:16:00 are 570 s apart, "so each turn earns some". That is what SM 2.8 gives and what `lookback/6.1-transcript-only` does: 120 s of `READ` in each of cycles 1 to 14, 1,680 s in all. Nothing is left to decide.

## 2. Codes for the lookback seconds that nothing credits

**Input.** SM 5.2 defines `X.absent` as "No presence: no input past the bridge and tail, lock, sleep" and `X.no_anchor` as "Present and focused, but no window covered the second". It lists `X.bucket_cap` with the caps of SM 2.4, and SM 2.8 says "The bucket cap drops to `UNKNOWN_BUCKET_MAX_S` = 120 s per turn". In lookback every slot's presence is `unknown` (SM 2.1).

**Expected.** Every second of a minute with a bucket has a code (SM 5.1).

**Computed.** A second inside an agent turn that nothing credits is `X.agent_only`: "An agent turn ran and nothing credited the second". The other uncredited seconds are of two kinds.

- **Cut by the cap.** The second lies between a stop and the human prompt that follows within 600 s, and the session would read it if the 120 s cap hadn't emptied its bucket. In 6.1 each turn fills 553 1/3 s, enough to read from the stop until the next prompt's lead, and the cap keeps 120 s of it. `X.bucket_cap` fits these seconds, and `pkg/event` defines it.
- **Uncovered.** The second lies outside every window and every such span. It fits neither `X.absent` nor `X.no_anchor`, because both say something about presence that lookback doesn't know.

| Case | Cut by the cap | Uncovered | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| `6.1-transcript-only` | 980 | 70 | Cut: 30 s after each of cycles 1 to 14's reading and 40 s before each of cycles 2 to 15's prompt lead, 14 × (30 + 40). Uncovered: the 40 s before cycle 1's lead and the 30 s after cycle 15's stop |
| `6.4-transcript-only` | 0 | 29 | No stop has a prompt within 600 s after it. 10 s before the `/goal` prompt's lead, 19 s before the follow-up's |
| `two-sessions` | 0 | 0 | |

In `two-sessions` the cap also cuts B's reading for 30 s while A's agent runs, 16:09:40 to 16:10:00 and 16:18:20 to 16:18:30. Those seconds are `X.agent_only` by its definition, and the fixture gives them that code. If the cap code should come first, they move too.

**What the fixture does.** Rows with either kind have `uncredited` null, name this erratum, and list `X.bucket_cap` for seconds cut by the cap and `X.no_anchor`, the proposal below, for uncovered ones, the way WP-FX1's 6.6 erratum handles codes SM doesn't decide. Scorer tests leave those rows' uncredited seconds unchecked. Their credit is checked as usual.

**For Karim.** Confirm `X.bucket_cap` for seconds the lookback cap cuts, and pick one for uncovered seconds:

- SM 5.2 says that in lookback `X.no_anchor` means "no lookback window or reading span covered the second", since nothing else is tested there.
- SM 5.2 adds a code, such as `X.unknown`. That also changes `pkg/event`'s reason list and the schema.

Either way the rows then get seconds.

## 3. Whose second is it when a typing lead meets another session

**Input.** SM 2.8: "Focus is unknown, so a second wanted by two parallel sessions goes to the one with the most recent human prompt."

**Expected.** The rule settles every second two sessions want.

**Computed.** For reading and permission windows it does. A second inside a prompt's typing lead is different, because that prompt hasn't been sent yet, so it isn't its session's most recent prompt. Read literally, the second goes to the other session whenever the other session prompted later, although the lead is there because the person was typing into the first. SM 3.2's rule for live terminal tabs looks ahead instead: "The slot goes to the session whose next human anchor in this window comes soonest, if the slot lies inside that anchor's lead."

An example. Take `two-sessions` and send B2 at 16:06:00 instead of 16:10:40, with the same 40 s lead, from 16:05:20. A1 stops at 16:05:00, and A reads from then. From 16:05:20 to 16:06:00 both want the second. The literal rule compares A1 at 16:02:00 with B1 at 16:01:00 and gives the 40 s to A's reading. Looking ahead, as SM 3.2 does, gives them to B's lead.

A related question: whether SM 3.3 rule 2, anchor windows before reading, applies across sessions before the recency rule does. In the case's one conflict between a permission window and reading, from 16:03:40, the permission's session also has the more recent prompt, so both answers give the same seconds.

**What the fixture does.** `two-sessions` keeps every typing lead clear of the other session's windows and reading, and gen.py checks that no contested second has a lead in it. Its contested seconds are 70 of A's permission window against B's reading and 210 of reading against reading, where both readings of the rule agree.

**For Karim.** Say whether a lead's seconds go to the session whose prompt they lead, as in SM 3.2, or follow the literal recency rule, and whether an anchor window beats another session's reading whatever their prompts' ages. Then a variant like the example above can join the fixtures.

## 4. Rule 4 needs fields a transcript may not have

**Input.** SM 2.8: "origin rules 1-5 still apply". Rule 4, `headless`, reads the session's `entrypoint` and `parent_class` and whether it has a TTY or an IDE or desktop-app host. SM 1.2 fills these from `SessionStart`, OTel and the hook binary's walk up the process tree. HS 3.2's list of transcript fields has `type`, `timestamp`, `sessionId`, `version`, `cwd`, `isSidechain` and the message, and none of those three.

**Computed.** If the log reader writes `entrypoint` `unknown`, `parent_class` `unknown` and no TTY, rule 4's last clause, "the session has neither a TTY nor an IDE or desktop-app host", makes every lookback prompt `headless`, and lookback credits nothing at all.

**What the fixture does.** The two SM cases keep their live `session.start` fields, `cli`, `terminal_app` and a TTY, as WP-FX5 asks ("the harness events of SM 6.1 and 6.4"), and the two-session case gives its sessions the same. Rule 4 then sees interactive sessions.

**For Karim.** Say what rule 4 does in lookback when the transcript can't tell, for example: skip the process-tree clauses and use only what the transcript states, such as an `sdk-*` entrypoint where it records one.

## 5. Does a permission answer keep its trail?

**Input.** SM 2.8: "Permission answers keep their wait, up to 60 s." SM 2.3 gives the same anchor a 10 s trail, `PA_TRAIL_S`. SM 2.1 says `unknown` slots are credited by "lookback rules only".

**Computed.** SM 2.8 reads as a list of changes to SM 2's rules. It changes the wait's cap and says nothing of the trail, so the trail stays. Read as the only things lookback may credit, the trail goes.

| Case | With the trail | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| `6.1-transcript-only` | 5,055 | 4,905 |
| `6.4-transcript-only` | 91 | 91 |
| `two-sessions` | 915 | 885 |

**What the fixture does.** It keeps the trail. The rows that change without it name this erratum.

**For Karim.** Confirm, or drop the trail and the rows change as above.

## Readings the fixtures treat as settled

- Reading drains from the stop, one second per second, as SM 2.4 drains a bucket, when the session wins the second.
- Two sessions are compared by their latest human prompts, as SM 2.8 says, not by their latest stops or their latest human acts. In `two-sessions` the three comparisons disagree from 16:13:30: A stopped later and answered a permission later, B prompted later, and B gets the 90 s.
- A permission wait over 60 s keeps its last 60 s before the answer, because a lead ends at its act (SM 2.3).
- A lead that isn't a whole number of seconds rounds up, as SM rounds 6.4's 160.67 s to 161. WP-FX1's 6.4 prompt-lengths erratum already asks which way edges round. Only 6.4's 07:36 row depends on it here.
- `COMPOSE_MAX_S` still caps the typing time. No prompt here is long enough for the cap to matter.

## Not reached by these fixtures

For whoever extends them, these are open too:

- A go-ahead's lead. `LOW_LEAD_S` has no typing part, so "typing time only" could mean about 1 s or the full 20.
- An answer's 60 s think part, which "no think lead" seems to drop, and an interrupt's 30 s lead and 10 s trail.
- "120 s per turn" when automated turns, which don't reset the bucket, add their output to a human turn's.
- An automated turn that starts between a stop and the next human prompt.
- Another session's prompt in the same project vouching for a stop's reading.
