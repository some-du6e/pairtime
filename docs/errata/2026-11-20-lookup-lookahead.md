# Erratum: SM 2.5's lookups look ahead without a bound

Date 2026-11-20. Raised by WP-SC2's review fix-up. Status: decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim: a lookup counts only the acts and prompts on record `READ_HORIZON_S` after its browser run starts, so a run still in front then, one whose return only a later act credits and one in which a turn of the project starts are no lookups. It is decided within the pending window, at most 780 s after the run's start with today's values, once the slot after the run has its presence and SM 4.2's rewrite rule has decided the discount of every rewrite candidate a bracket's credit rests on (Decision, below). No fixture's output changes. It follows on from [the return-rule look-ahead erratum](2026-11-19-return-rule-lookahead.md), which asks how far ahead of a second a rule may look before the second's minute is final, and it was raised by the #52 review (#80).

## Input

- DESIGN 3.7 and 6.2 D1: a minute bucket stays `pending` for `PENDING_WINDOW_S` = 900 s after its minute ends, then it is `final`.
- SM 2.5: a run of browser or chat-app slots counts as `LOOKUP` for project `p` when it "starts within `LOOKUP_BRACKET_S` = 30 s after a credited slot of `p`, ends within 30 s before a credited slot of `p`, and no agent turn in `p` is running during it". The run is credited up to `LOOKUP_MAX_S` = 180 s.

## Expected

Every rule that can credit a second has decided it by the time the second's minute is final, as the return-rule erratum asks.

## What the rules give

SM puts no bound on how long the run is, so whether its first second is credited is decided only at its end. A two-hour video right after a credited slot is a lookup if the person comes back to the project within 30 s of its end, and its first 180 s are then `LOOKUP`, credited two hours after they happened. That reaches further than the pending window and further than the return rule's 1,200 s.

## What the scorer does

`pkg/score` holds `FinalBefore` at the start of such a run until its end bracket is final, so a Step per minute agrees with one Step over the same records. It holds only for runs with a credited second in the `LOOKUP_BRACKET_S` before them: once a run's start is final, that bracket is too, and a run with nothing there can't be a lookup whatever follows it. For a run that does have one, the hold has no bound, and WP-SC4's pending rule needs one.

## For Karim

Alongside the three rules the return-rule erratum offers:

1. Cap the run: a browser or chat-app run longer than some bound, for example `PENDING_WINDOW_S`, is no lookup. SM 8.3 already calls `LOOKUP_MAX_S` "a documentation lookup, not an evening of video".
2. Or keep the hold, and let a lookup's minutes be pending for as long as its run lasts.
3. Or let a lookup's credit land in a later revision of a final bucket, as that erratum's third rule would for the return rule.

## Decision

Decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim, with a new rule: option 1's bound, put on what is on record rather than on the run's length. A lookup has `READ_HORIZON_S` (600 s) from its run's start, as the return rule has from the start of a silence (SM 2.1), and counts only the acts and prompts on record by then. By then the run must have ended, and the slots before and after it must be credited counting only the acts on record by then, an act being on record at the later of its own time and its event's `t`, and a relayed message once it pairs. No agent turn of the project may start or run during the run: a prompt of the project sent during it makes it no lookup, even when its turn's first agent record comes later or never, as a plugin's, the bridge's or a `track` call's can (SM 1.3). A turn that began before the run is running at its start, or reaches into it only through an agent record within `READ_HORIZON_S` of the one before, which is on record by the horizon too. An act on record later can't make a run a lookup or stop it being one, and neither can a turn whose first record comes later, such as one a stop hook continues from a stop inside the run. So a run still in front 600 s after it starts is no lookup, whatever follows it, and neither is one whose return only a later act credits. Their present seconds are `X.other_focus`, as in any browser run that isn't a lookup, and no minute waits for a run's end. The lookup is decided once two more things settle, though neither adds an act or a prompt to it. One is the presence of the slot after the run, which can rest on input up to `PRESENCE_GAP_S` later and on the sample that reports it, which can come up to `SLEEP_GAP_S` after the sample before it with no hard break (SM 2.1), so it settles before `READ_HORIZON_S` + `LOOKUP_BRACKET_S` + `PRESENCE_GAP_S` + `SLEEP_GAP_S` = 780 s after the run's start. The other is whether SM 4.2's rewrite rule discounts each rewrite candidate a bracket's credit rests on, whichever bracket and however it rests on it: through the candidate's lead, through its prompt being the only anchor that qualifies the bracket's reading (SM 2.4), or through its stop, which a later lead can't reach back past once the turn is discounted. The rule reads each such turn's agent edits on record within `READ_HORIZON_S` - `THINK_LEAD_S` - `COMPOSE_MAX_S` = 180 s after its prompt, which is on record by the horizon, so it decides at most 2 × `READ_HORIZON_S` - `THINK_LEAD_S` - `COMPOSE_MAX_S` = 780 s after the run's start. Agent edits aren't acts, but later ones can still decide the discount of a prompt the lookup counts. For a teen: a browser visit between two stretches of project work counts, for at most three minutes, only when your agent there isn't started or working during it and what you do back in the project is recorded within ten minutes of leaving it. No parameter is added and no value changes.

So every act and prompt a lookup counts is on record within 600 s of its run's start, and every lookup is decided within the pending window, at most 780 s after it with today's values. That is the reach DESIGN 3.7 already names for reading and return credit, inside the pending window with 120 s left for late records. `pkg/score` drops Step's hold. CC6's range, which runs `REVEAL_POST_S` = 900 s past the window, reaches everything a window's lookup rests on.

WP-SC5's checkpoints hold what a pending lookup rests on. A later record can still credit any second not yet final at a checkpoint, take over its reading as its nearest anchor, qualify reading drained there or end a turn in it, as at SM 6.1's 09:15, 10:15 and 11:15 UTC checkpoints, where the reading just before each browser run is nearest the edit or commit after it. So a checkpoint holds, from the records numbered below it, what the credit pass, the presence rule and the turn test read for every second from the first one not yet final to the checkpoint, and every browser or chat-app run in that stretch that nothing on record has ruled out in either scoring, whether it is in front at the checkpoint or ended before it. WP-SC5's check that a scorer resumed from a checkpoint gives what one Step gives is the oracle for what that holds in code. Its CI cases name the shapes that need it: a checkpoint inside a browser run; one just before a run whose start bracket only a later act credits; one a minute or more after a run ended but before the act that credits its end bracket; and one after that act but before the input that settles the presence of the slot after the run. The case of a rewrite candidate a bracket rests on comes with WP-SC6, which builds the rewrite rule.

The arithmetic above holds, with three notes. The two-hour video's first second waits longer than two hours: the end bracket's own credit can rest on an act up to `READ_HORIZON_S` after the bracket, so up to 2 h 10 min 30 s. The return rule's 1,200 s became 600 s from the start of the silence under E-return-lookahead. And the hold covered the turn test too: whether a turn of the project ran during the run can wait for a side door's first agent record, up to `READ_HORIZON_S` after a prompt sent in the run, so a bound on the brackets alone would have let that record change a minute already final. The hold is on `FinalBefore`, so while a bracketed run stays in front every later minute of every project stays pending, not only the run's. That is why #235's stall warning stays silent for a browser in front, and the code half drops that exception with the hold.

Why the options lost. 1 as written: a run capped at `PENDING_WINDOW_S` can end 900 s after its first second and its end bracket 30 s later. The bracket's credit can rest on an act up to 600 s after that, or 780 s for a rewrite candidate, so the first second waits up to 1,530 s (1,710 s). A cap on length alone would have to be under about 90 s, below SM 6.1's 120 s runs and `LOOKUP_MAX_S`. 2: the hold has no bound and keeps every project's minutes pending. It leaves WP-SC5's pending state open for as long as a browser stays in front, and `REVEAL_POST_S` can't reach the end bracket. 3: a final bucket that changes later isn't final. Crediting a long run's last 180 s instead of its first would bound the credit, but it would still pay an evening of video 180 s, and the code of the run's earlier seconds would wait for its end.

What it costs: a browser run whose return isn't on record within ten minutes of its start loses the at most 180 s SM gave it. Examples are a 12-minute manual test of a web app, or a five-minute check followed by six minutes of typing a prompt. So does a run in which a turn of the project starts through a plugin, the bridge or a `track` call before its agent shows anything. An evening of video between two credited slots loses its 180 s too. One case goes the other way: a later act of another project that takes the slot after the run, as SM 3.2 rule 1's look-ahead can, no longer cancels the lookup, whose second anchor is then the act that credited that slot by the horizon. It keeps at most `LOOKUP_MAX_S`, with acts of the project on both sides, so it gives nothing to farm. No fixture moves, and no fixture has a prompt or an agent record during a browser run. SM 6.1's 14 runs are decided by the hand edit's first heartbeat or the commit, 122 to 160 s after each run starts. Every browser run in SM 6.5 is decided within 180 s of its start, the latest by T21's prompt after the 20:46 run. `pkg/score`'s 240 s table-test run, bracketed by a heartbeat 270 s after it starts, keeps `LOOKUP` 180 and `X.lookup_cap` 60, and SM 6.3's illustrative five minutes of `X.lookup_cap` can come from runs of four to nine minutes.

A scoring rule changes in SM and in code in the same PR (AGENTS.md), so the code half (`pkg/score` and `internal/agent/scorer`) carries SM 2.5, 8.3 and Risk 4, which state the rule, DESIGN 3.7, 6.2 D1 and 6.5, which state the reach and the checkpoint state, and PLAN's WP-SC2, WP-SC4, WP-SC5 and WP-SC6, in one PR with the `oracle-change` label, after #234 and before or with WP-SC5 part 1. That PR raises `pkg/score/VERSION` to the next MINOR at its merge and moves `BuiltinScoringVersion` with it.
