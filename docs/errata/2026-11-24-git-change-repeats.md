# Erratum: a git change scanned again after a crash goes to the ledger twice, and CC4's oracle refuses what a device sends for it

Date 2026-11-24. Raised by WP-AG5's plan for the commit-record upload. Status: decided 2026-11-24 by the orchestrator (D-ag5-C1). Two halves are still to come. Code half: a WP-AG5 follow-up after WP-AG8. The git host gives a change an event_id from its place (the worktree's key and its reflog entry) instead of `git.go`'s random one, and leaves out a change the adapters' replay index holds. Oracle half: `cc4` in WP-FX8's next testdata/checks PR, the one that takes #338's items, under the `oracle-change` label if it touches code.

## Input

- D-late-local point 5 ([the late-local erratum](2026-11-23-late-local-records.md), Decision 5): "Where a tailer or adapter replay (Rewind, relocate, a restart's resume, a crash re-read) re-emits an event the ledger already holds, the adapter drops it, so nothing counts twice." A log reader's or a hook call's record gets its event_id from its place (`adapterHost.logEventID`, `adapterHost.hookEventID`), and the adapters leave out one whose event_id their replay index holds (`heldLogs`).
- The git host's sink (`gitHost.sink` in `internal/agent/git.go`) appends a change's commit records to `commits/` (`commitStore.add`), writes its git.change, whose event_id is a UUIDv7 of its `t` with random other bits (`event.NewEventID`), seals it, and then asks for a save of `state.json`, which holds the watcher's cursor (`saveSoon`). `commitStore`'s comment says what a crash in between does: "A crash after the records went and before the cursor did writes them again at the next start, so a sha can have more than one line."
- A scan reads the clock once, as it begins (`Scanner.Scan`), and that time is its git.change's `t` and its commit records' `observed_at`.
- pkg/score counts a commit once. `projectCommits` orders the records by `t`, then `seq` (`recordCmp`), and keeps the first git.change of each project and sha.
- DESIGN 7.3's CC4 row: "`observed_at` before `server_first_seen_at` before `observed_at` plus 15 minutes; on App-installed repos, push time not earlier than `observed_at` minus 60 s; forge SHA and diffstat match". Its oracle, `checks.py`'s `cc4`, takes each git.change of op `commit` in the submission's projects and window, looks up the server's record of its sha, checks that the record's `observed_at` is the git.change's `t`, and matches the forge's diffstat against the git.change's counts.
- The upload of the commit records isn't on main yet, and WP-AG5's rulings decide it. The uploader sends a sha at most once per device, as the first line `commits/` holds for it (D-ag5-D2), and withholds some lines whose batches can still be submitted (D-ag5-D1). A commit whose shortstat wasn't read leaves its counts out of its record (D-ag5-D4, #340), while its git.change says 0/0/0, since pkg/event's counts aren't optional.

## Expected

A scan that reads a git change again, after the ledger took it, makes no second git.change. Nothing counts twice (D-late-local point 5). CC4's oracle judges the commit records a real device sends, each commit once.

## What the code does

On main at f6b05908, take one commit, whose lines in `commits/` are L1 and L2 and whose git.changes are G1 and G2. The sink appends L1 and seals G1 at 14:00:00, and the power goes before `saveSoon` moves the cursor. The machine boots with its clock an hour behind, as a dead CMOS battery or a dual boot that keeps the RTC in local time gives, and the catch-up scan writes L2 and G2 at 13:05 in the same day file, after L1. G2 gets a random event_id of its own, so nothing ties it to G1. Until the code half lands, the ledger keeps two records of one commit, and a reveal shows both.

pkg/score's commit is G2, D-ag5-D2 sends L1, and 14:00:00 is later than G2's `t`. `recordCmp` orders by `t`, then `seq`, while D-ag5-D2 orders by day file and file order. `cc4`'s check that `c["observed_at"] == e["t"]` rejects such input, and its `by_sha` lookup assumes a record for every git.change, while D-ag5-D1 withholds some. The check fails with no step of the clock too, when the crash falls between `commitStore.add` and the ledger's `Write`. The change's one git.change is then the re-scan's, later than L1. And a commit whose shortstat wasn't read fails its forge match, since its git.change says 0/0/0. Until the oracle half lands, the oracle refuses real devices' data.

## Decision

Decided 2026-11-24 by the orchestrator (D-ag5-C1), outside WP-AG5's PRs:

1. A crash re-scan's second git.change of a change the ledger already holds falls under D-late-local point 5, applied to the git watcher. #230 and #290 built that point only for log readers and hook calls. The git host gives a change an event_id from its place (the worktree's key and its reflog entry) instead of `git.go`'s random one, and leaves out a change the adapters' replay index holds. WP-LO1 built that index, and a WP-AG5 follow-up after WP-AG8 builds this.
2. CC4's oracle (`checks.py`'s `cc4`, in the testdata/checks batch that WP-TR1 reads) counts each project's sha once, at its earliest git.change, as pkg/score's `projectCommits` does. It reads the server's record for that sha without comparing its `observed_at` with that git.change's `t`, and judges seen-in-order by the record's own `observed_at`. The record is the first sighting `commits/` holds. It is earlier than that `t` when a crash fell between `commitStore.add` and `Write`, and later after a step back of the wall clock between two sightings, or when another clone's sighting reached the sink first. The oracle takes a commit with no record on the server as not computable for its clock half, because D-ag5-D1 withholds some records whose batches can still be submitted: near switches, and in a stretch whose edges share no monotonic clock. And it reads the diffstat from the commit record, absent when unread (D-ag5-D4), because the git.change says 0/0/0 then. WP-FX8's next testdata/checks PR, the one that takes #338's items, makes this change, under the `oracle-change` label if it touches code.

WP-AG5's upload needs neither: D-ag5-D2's first line is the device's first recorded sighting, and the sink's order, lines before `Write`, keeps it at or before its own git.change's `t`.

The tests: in `internal/agent`, "a git change scanned again after a kill before its cursor was saved makes no second git.change"; in testdata/checks, `cc4` cases with a repeated git.change whose second record has the earlier `t`, and with a NoStat commit. In WP-AG5, `TestCommitPlan` has D-ag5-D2's two re-scan cases: "a crash re-scan's second line of a sha, with a later observed_at and actor unknown, never goes" and "a crash re-scan's second line of a sha with an earlier observed_at than the first, after a step back of the wall clock, never goes".
