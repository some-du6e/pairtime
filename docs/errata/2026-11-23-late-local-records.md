# Erratum: a record that reaches the ledger late stops a linked computer's uploads

Date 2026-11-23. Raised by a stop of the uploads on a linked test computer, investigated by the orchestrator the same day. Status: decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim (D-late-local). Points 1 to 4 are built by WP-LO1's first PR, #224, with this erratum and DESIGN 3.6's and 4.2's wording below. Point 5, an adapter's replays, is built by #230 for a log reader's records, with DESIGN 3.6's replay rule below. The review of #224 (#238) changed two readings: the stretches fold at a retention sweep, as DESIGN 4.4's ledger row doesn't cover them, and a stretch whose edges share no monotonic clock holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the stretch before it.

## Input

- DESIGN 4.2: "Hours logged in this mode are unwitnessed and can never be submitted."
- docs/spec/ingest-v1.md 5.3: batches sealed in local-only mode never go, and the server takes a skip only at a re-link, so a batch sealed local-only after the link's first stored batch stops the batch roots until `pairtime logout`, then `pairtime login`.
- #174's rule (docs/PLAN.md, WP-AG5): the ledger writer keeps the moment `config.toml` was last known to say local-only, by each clock, and seals any batch that holds an event made before it local-only, whenever the event comes.
- #177's `Writer.lookback`: a record a log reader read, dated before the chain began, counts by when the writer took it in instead of its own time.

## Expected

A record made while `config.toml` said local-only never uploads, and a record made while it said online never stops a linked computer's uploads, however late either reaches the ledger.

## Computed

At 04:22 UTC the test computer's agent, v0.1.0-alpha.5, linked and online, sealed batch 4269 local-only. The batch held two Claude Code records that a log reader read again from an old transcript, an `agent.output` and an `agent.stop` dated 2026-11-18, four days before the chain began, with a `run-` boot_id and mono 0, beside the live hook records of that minute. The writer judged the two by their own time, which was before the last moment `config.toml` said local-only, the link at 2026-11-22 19:53 UTC, so their batch went local-only. The uploader stopped at it, and doctor said to log out and back in, which the orchestrator did at 08:25. Batches 4269 to 5165, about four hours of agent time, are a recorded gap now, and never upload.

alpha.6's `Writer.lookback` covers that case. It doesn't cover the rule itself: one bound for every record. After any logout and login, or any local-only stretch, the moment is recent, and a record dated after the chain began but before that moment, which reaches the writer late, still seals a local-only batch after online ones. The investigation found about 19 ways a record reaches the writer late in alpha.6: a tailer's Rewind of a transcript it has no adapter memory of, a relocate of a file rewritten in place, a crash's re-read of the lines since the last save, a restart's replay of a held final or start, a file first seen after its folder's first listing, a forked session's copied history, live lines held behind a lookback history, the process scan's session.start, hook calls that escape `Waiting`, and the clocks. Most of them carry records made while `config.toml` said online.

## Decision

Decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim (D-late-local):

1. The writer judges a record dated before the last local-only moment by whether its own time falls inside a local-only stretch the writer knows, not by that single bound. A stretch runs from when NoteMode, Seen or a seal's read first saw `config.toml` local-only to when it was seen online again. The writer keeps its stretches across restarts for the chain's life. A record dated in an online stretch seals online, however late it arrives.
2. A record dated inside a local-only stretch that reaches the writer after a batch was sealed online since that stretch ended is not written to the ledger. It is dropped, counted, and never uploaded, and the writer never seals a local-only batch after an online one because of it. Waiting's rule stays: a record known to be on its way is sealed local-only before the first online batch after the stretch.
3. Records dated before the chain began keep `Writer.lookback`'s rule.
4. When the roots do stop at a local-only batch, doctor's `problem.uploader` names the real cause: which batch, and the kinds and dates of the records that made it local-only, not "a hook call that waited". Doctor also counts dropped late local records.
5. Where a tailer or adapter replay (Rewind, relocate, a restart's resume, a crash re-read) re-emits an event the ledger already holds, the adapter drops it, so nothing counts twice.

DESIGN 4.2's local-only row, in its What column, becomes:

> `mode = "local"` in `config.toml`: nothing is uploaded, no roots, no buckets, no profile. The coach, recaps and chess clock still work. Hours logged in this mode are unwitnessed and can never be submitted. A record made in a local-only stretch counts as logged in it however late it reaches the ledger: while no batch has been sealed online since the stretch ended, it joins the stretch's local-only batches, and after that the ledger drops it, counts it, and never uploads it. A stretch starts at the last moment the writer knew `config.toml` said online, and a record no stretch holds counts as online however late it comes (3.6; docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md)

DESIGN 3.6 gets a rule after the upload-failure notes:

> - **A batch's mode.** Each seal frame records whether its batch was sealed online and, for one sealed local-only, why: `config.toml` said local-only as it was sealed, a record came while it did, something made in a local-only stretch was still on its way, or the batch holds records of a stretch that came late, named by their kinds and their own times, never their content. The writer keeps every local-only stretch it knows in the seal frames, from the last moment it knew `config.toml` said online to the moment it saw the file online again, and judges a record that reaches it late by the record's own time against them. A stretch whose edges share no monotonic clock, as one left open across a reboot, holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the stretch before it. A retention sweep folds every stretch that began before its day into one from the beginning of time to that day or to the latest end among them, whichever is later, or into one still going on while one of them does, which only makes more of the past count as local-only (4.4). A record a log reader read (`via = log`), dated before the chain began and with no reading of a run's clock, counts by when the writer took it in; any other record counts by its own time (4.2; docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md).

#230 adds one after it:

> - **A replayed log line.** A log reader's record whose event_id a batch sealed in the 48 hours before the previous run's last seal, or a batch sealed after it, as Open's of what a kill left, holds, or a record this run wrote in the last 48 hours, is dropped and counted as a repeat, so a crash's re-read, a restart's resume, a Rewind or a relocate makes no second record of it (docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md).

## What the code does

- **Stretches.** `internal/agent/ledger` keeps a list of stretches, each with a start, an end while it isn't going on, whether a batch was sealed online since it ended, and how many of its records it dropped. A stretch begins where the writer last knew `config.toml` said online before it took the file as local-only: a seal's read that found it online, taken just before the read, or the end of the stretch before. The switch came between that moment and the first local-only sighting, so the stretch takes in the time between, as privacy wins what can't be told (#174). This is wider than point 1's wording, which starts at the first local-only sighting. The chain's first stretch begins at the beginning of time, since every install starts in local mode. Each edge holds its own millisecond and is read by the clocks a moment holds: this run's monotonic clock, the OS's of its boot, and the wall clock otherwise, with an edge an earlier run recorded in another boot keeping that boot's reading for its hook calls. When a stretch ends and its edges span less by the wall clock than by a monotonic clock both hold, the wall clock stepped back inside it. A record of it that is judged by its t alone, as a log record read late, can carry any time the wall clock showed, so both edges move out by the step. When its edges share no monotonic clock, as for a stretch left open across a reboot, one that began at a moment Open read back from a row, or one rebuilt from an older version's rows, nothing tells how far the wall clock stepped, so the stretch holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the stretch before it, or from the beginning of time for the first, as #174's single moment held every record before it.
- **Writing a late record.** `Writer.Write` judges an event by its own times and its sender's stamp, and a lookback record, one a log reader read that is dated before the chain began and carries no reading of a run's clock, by when the writer took it in. Any other record dated before the chain began, as a hook call held through the link, counts by its own time, so the chain's first stretch holds it. One a stretch that ended holds is sealed local-only while no batch has been sealed online since, and otherwise dropped with `ErrLateLocal`, before it takes a sequence number. One the stretch going on holds is left to that stretch's taint and end, as before, and one no stretch holds goes online. A record already pending, which a stretch end the writer hears of later holds, keeps its number and makes its batch local-only.
- **Waiting.** A seal asks `Options.Waiting` about the stretches that ended and no batch sealed online has passed, with the one going on ended at the seal's read when that read ends it. A record on its way from a passed stretch would be dropped when it comes, so it holds no mark. The adapters keep only the earliest stamp of the calls held in held.jsonl, which tells whether one came before a moment but not whether one came inside a stretch, so while any held call's times could be read they count whenever Waiting holds any stretch, as calls not yet counted do, at the cost of credit while they drain after a switch back. Held calls that can't reach the writer in this run, behind a .replay file it can't delete or a writer that stopped after a failure, wait for the next start and count for nothing.
- **Persistence.** The seal frame of each batch sealed after the stretches changed records all of them, and its row in `batches.seg` does too. Open takes them back from the newest seal frame a crash left without a row, or the newest row, or the base frame a retention sweep writes, which keeps the newest list when the sweep drops the row that had it. A ledger whose seal frames recorded none, as an older version's, gets them rebuilt from its rows' `local_until`: each rise of it ends a stretch, which begins where the one before it ended, as no row records a moment by a monotonic clock where `config.toml` was seen local-only. Rows sealed after the newest one that records the stretches, with none of their own, as an older version seals them after a downgrade, are read the same way: the first rise of their `local_until` past the newest list's end ends the stretch that list has going on, passed once a batch was sealed online from that row on, and each later rise ends a stretch of its own. This version records the list in the next seal frame whenever its `local_until` moves, so a row whose moment rose with no list is an older version's. A sweep that moves the list into its base frame keeps there the moment the list's row recorded, and Open holds the rows kept after the frame to it the same way. A stretch the newest seal frame recorded as going on still goes on at Open, until a note, the agent's `Seen` or a seal's read that finds the file online ends it. The writer keeps at most 64 stretches and merges the oldest two past that, which only makes more of the past count as local-only. They hold when `config.toml` changed mode, never content. DESIGN 4.4's row for the device's ledger records and batch roots doesn't cover them, so a retention sweep folds them at the start of its day: every stretch that began before it becomes part of one from the beginning of time to that day or to the latest end among them, whichever is later, or one going on while one of them does, passed when all of them are, with all their drops, in the writer and in the base frame the sweep writes, and the next seal frame records them so. That only makes more of the past count as local-only, and the stretches the writer and `batches.seg`'s base frame keep hold no time the mode changed before the ledger's 180 days.
- **Stretches the agent saw.** A stretch end the agent gives (`NoteSeen`, `Options.Seen`) ends the stretch going on, as before. One that ends nothing the writer has, being later than every end it knows or taken in a boot no end of the writer's is of, is recorded as a stretch from where the writer last knew the file said online. When its end is of a boot that start holds no reading of, every hook call of that boot counts as made after the start, whatever that boot's wall clock said. It counts as passed when a batch was sealed online after it, as far as the clocks tell, and never for an end of another boot, which no clock orders with the writer's batches.
- **Why a batch is local-only.** The seal frame of a local-only batch records `why`: `config` when `Options.Online` said no, `noted` when a record came while the writer took the file as local-only, `waiting`, and `late`, the first four late records by kind and time, with how many there were. `Batch.Why` and `Row.Why` carry it. The uploader's `problem.uploader` names the batch the roots stop at, when it was sealed, and these causes, and `pairtime privacy show` adds the same line. A batch an older version sealed keeps the general text, without "a hook call that waited".
- **Counting the drops.** The writer counts dropped records in their stretches, so the count survives restarts (`Status.LateLocal`), and `GET /v1/status` gives it as `ledger.late_local`, which `pairtime doctor` prints as a line of its own. It counts every caller's drops, the adapters', presence's and the git watcher's. The adapter host counts its harness's since the agent started in `late_local`, which `pairtime doctor`'s adapter lines print as the breakdown, and the agent's log names each drop's kind and time, presence's and the git watcher's too. A drop is not a ledger refusal, and none of its callers reports it as a problem.
- **Replays (point 5, #230).** A log reader's record gets its event_id from its line's file and place (`adapterHost.logEventID`), so a replay that reads the line again makes the same event_id. At a run's first log line, the adapters read the event_ids of the records in every batch sealed from 48 hours before the newest batch an earlier run sealed, which the writer names by its number as Open found the ledger (`ledger.Writer.Opened`), and in every batch after it, as Open's of what a kill left (`heldLogs`). The anchor goes by its number, so a start clock that reads behind changes nothing. The walk back from it stops at a batch sealed more than 48 hours after it, as when that run's clock was reset to an old year, and reads at most 10,000 batches, so a clock that stepped between the seals can't make it read the whole ledger. A record whose event_id that set holds, or that the adapters wrote in this run, is left out and counted as `repeats`. A record the ledger dropped earlier in this run as made in a local-only stretch is left out of a same-run replay too, and counts as neither a repeat nor a second drop. The ids read from the ledger stay for the run's life, as do those the ledger dropped in this run, and those this run wrote for 48 hours.

## Known limits

Accepted by the orchestrator on 2026-11-23 in the fix-verify of #224 (#238):

- A seal frame keeps the stretch list it was sealed with until its day's segment is deleted, so a time the mode changed can stay in the seal frames of the newest 180 days for up to 180 days after the sweep that folded it. The frame sits in its day's append-only segment, and `batches.seg`'s rows point into it by offset, so rewriting it would move every later frame. DESIGN 4.4's ledger row covers the frame and what it holds.
- `local_until`, the newest stretch end, stays in every row and seal frame however old it is (#174), and in the base frame a sweep writes, as the moment the list's row recorded. Folding it touches the downgrade guard (`restoreStretches`), the uploader's straddle rule and the rebuild of state.json's modes, so it goes to a follow-up after #224, which folds the base frame's copy too.

Accepted by the orchestrator on 2026-11-23 in the review of #230 (#269):

- A record sealed more than 48 hours before the run before's last seal, or more than 10,000 batches before it, which a Rewind or a relocate brings back, goes to the ledger again, and so does this run's own record written more than 48 hours earlier. pkg/score drops it for the one it holds if that one is still in its state, and a minute it already made final stays final.
- A relocate whose lines moved gives them new offsets, so new event_ids, which no index by event_id matches.
- A hook call parsed again, as after a crash during a held.jsonl replay or a kill just after its records were written, comes out twice, under new random event_ids. #268 makes those ids deterministic and checks them against the same index.
- Which records the ledger dropped as made in local-only mode stays in memory only, so a line read again after a restart reaches the writer, which drops it and counts it again.

## Still open

- A stretch end with no batch sealed after it reaches no seal frame. After a restart the agent's `Seen` gives it, and without that the stretch reads as going on, so it ends at the first read that finds `config.toml` online, later than it did. That is wider, and costs only credit.
- A record of a stretch that comes before any batch was sealed online after it still makes a local-only batch, as point 2 keeps Waiting's rule. After a `config.toml` edit back online, with no sign-in, that batch stops the roots, as any record of that stretch would have (ingest-v1 5.3). After a sign-in it falls in the re-link's gap.
- As #174's Still open says of a step of the wall clock where an event and the moment share no monotonic clock: a stretch whose edges share none holds what the wall clock alone dates from the end of the stretch before it, so a step back longer than the time between the two stretches can still date such a record before every stretch it was made in, and two steps inside one run that cancel out leave its edges' spans equal, so the stretch isn't widened. Such a record seals online.
- The count of drops reaches disk only with a seal frame, so the drops after a run's last batch are left out of it after a restart.
- Commit records don't upload yet. A git change the writer drops still has its commit records in `commits/`, and they need the same rule once the uploader sends them, as #174 says.
