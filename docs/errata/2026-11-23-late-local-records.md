# Erratum: a record that reaches the ledger late stops a linked computer's uploads

Date 2026-11-23. Raised by a stop of the uploads on a linked test computer, investigated by the orchestrator the same day. Status: decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim (D-late-local). Points 1 to 4 are built by WP-LO1's first PR, with this erratum and DESIGN 3.6's and 4.2's wording below. Point 5, an adapter's replays, follows in a second PR.

## Input

- DESIGN 4.2: "Hours logged in this mode are unwitnessed and can never be submitted."
- docs/spec/ingest-v1.md 5.3: batches sealed in local-only mode never go, and the server takes a skip only at a re-link, so a batch sealed local-only after the link's first stored batch stops the batch roots until `pairtime logout`, then `pairtime login`.
- #174's rule (docs/PLAN.md, WP-AG5): the ledger writer keeps the moment `config.toml` was last known to say local-only, by each clock, and seals any batch that holds an event made before it local-only, whenever the event comes.
- #177's `Writer.lookback`: a record a log reader read, dated before the chain began, counts by when the writer took it in instead of its own time.

## Expected

A record made while `config.toml` said local-only never uploads, and a record made while it said online never stops a linked computer's uploads, however late either reaches the ledger.

## Computed

At 04:22 UTC the test computer's agent, v0.1.0-alpha.5, linked and online, sealed batch 4269 local-only. The batch held two Claude Code records that a log reader read again from an old transcript, an `agent.output` and an `agent.stop` dated 2026-11-18, four days before the chain began, with a `run-` boot_id and mono 0, beside the live hook records of that minute. The writer judged the two by their own time, which was before the last moment `config.toml` said local-only, the link at 2026-11-22 19:53 UTC, so their batch went local-only. The uploader stopped at it, and doctor said to log out and back in, which the orchestrator did at 08:25. Batches 4269 to 5165, about four hours of agent time, are a recorded gap now, and never upload.

alpha.6's `Writer.lookback` covers that case. It doesn't cover the rule itself: one bound for every record. After any logout and login, or any local-only stretch, the moment is recent, and a record dated after the chain began but before that moment, which reaches the writer late, still seals a local-only batch after online ones. The investigation found about 19 ways a record reaches the writer late in alpha.6: a tailer's Rewind of a transcript it has no adapter memory of, a relocate of a file rewritten in place, a crash's re-read of the lines since the last save, a restart's replay of a held final or start, a file first seen after its folder's first listing, a forked session's copied history, live lines held behind a lookback history, the process scan's session.start, hook calls that escape `Waiting`, and the clocks. Most of them carry records made while `config.toml` said online.

## Decision

Decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim (D-late-local):

1. The writer judges a record dated before the last local-only moment by whether its own time falls inside a local-only stretch the writer knows, not by that single bound. A stretch runs from when NoteMode, Seen or a seal's read first saw `config.toml` local-only to when it was seen online again. The writer keeps its stretches across restarts for the chain's life. A record dated in an online stretch seals online, however late it arrives.
2. A record dated inside a local-only stretch that reaches the writer after a batch was sealed online since that stretch ended is not written to the ledger. It is dropped, counted, and never uploaded, and the writer never seals a local-only batch after an online one because of it. Waiting's rule stays: a record known to be on its way is sealed local-only before the first online batch after the stretch.
3. Records dated before the chain began keep `Writer.lookback`'s rule.
4. When the roots do stop at a local-only batch, doctor's `problem.uploader` names the real cause: which batch, and the kinds and dates of the records that made it local-only, not "a hook call that waited". Doctor also counts dropped late local records.
5. Where a tailer or adapter replay (Rewind, relocate, a restart's resume, a crash re-read) re-emits an event the ledger already holds, the adapter drops it, so nothing counts twice.

DESIGN 4.2's local-only row, in its What column, becomes:

> `mode = "local"` in `config.toml`: nothing is uploaded, no roots, no buckets, no profile. The coach, recaps and chess clock still work. Hours logged in this mode are unwitnessed and can never be submitted. A record made in a local-only stretch counts as logged in it however late it reaches the ledger: while no batch has been sealed online since the stretch ended, it joins the stretch's local-only batches, and after that the ledger drops it, counts it, and never uploads it. A record made while `config.toml` said online counts as online however late it comes (3.6; docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md)

DESIGN 3.6 gets a rule after the upload-failure notes:

> - **A batch's mode.** Each seal frame records whether its batch was sealed online and, for one sealed local-only, why: `config.toml` said local-only as it was sealed, a record came while it did, something made in a local-only stretch was still on its way, or the batch holds records of a stretch that came late, named by their kinds and their own times, never their content. The writer keeps every local-only stretch it knows in the seal frames, from the last moment it knew `config.toml` said online to the moment it saw the file online again, and judges a record that reaches it late by the record's own time against them. A record dated before the chain began counts by when the writer took it in (4.2; docs/errata/2026-11-23-late-local-records.md).

## What the code does

- **Stretches.** `internal/agent/ledger` keeps a list of stretches, each with a start, an end while it isn't going on, whether a batch was sealed online since it ended, and how many of its records it dropped. A stretch begins where the writer last knew `config.toml` said online before it took the file as local-only: a seal's read that found it online, taken just before the read, or the end of the stretch before. The switch came between that moment and the first local-only sighting, so the stretch takes in the time between, as privacy wins what can't be told (#174). This is wider than point 1's wording, which starts at the first local-only sighting. The chain's first stretch begins at the beginning of time, since every install starts in local mode. Each edge holds its own millisecond and is read by the clocks a moment holds: this run's monotonic clock, the OS's of its boot, and the wall clock otherwise, with an edge an earlier run recorded in another boot keeping that boot's reading for its hook calls.
- **Writing a late record.** `Writer.Write` judges an event by its own times and its sender's stamp, and a lookback record by when the writer took it in. One a stretch that ended holds is sealed local-only while no batch has been sealed online since, and otherwise dropped with `ErrLateLocal`, before it takes a sequence number. One the stretch going on holds is left to that stretch's taint and end, as before, and one no stretch holds goes online. A record already pending, which a stretch end the writer hears of later holds, keeps its number and makes its batch local-only.
- **Waiting.** A seal asks `Options.Waiting` about the stretches that ended and no batch sealed online has passed, with the one going on ended at the seal's read when that read ends it. A record on its way from a passed stretch would be dropped when it comes, so it holds no mark.
- **Persistence.** The seal frame of each batch sealed after the stretches changed records all of them, and its row in `batches.seg` does too. Open takes them back from the newest seal frame a crash left without a row, or the newest row, or the base frame a retention sweep writes, which keeps the newest list when the sweep drops the row that had it. A ledger whose seal frames recorded none, as an older version's, gets them rebuilt from its rows' `local_until`: each rise of it ends a stretch, which begins at the seal of the batch before the newest one sealed online before that end, since that batch's read of `config.toml` came after that seal. A stretch the newest seal frame recorded as going on still goes on at Open, until a note, the agent's `Seen` or a seal's read that finds the file online ends it. The writer keeps at most 64 stretches and merges the oldest two past that, which only makes more of the past count as local-only.
- **Stretches the agent saw.** A stretch end the agent gives (`NoteSeen`, `Options.Seen`) ends the stretch going on, as before. One that ends nothing the writer has, being later than every end it knows or taken in a boot no end of the writer's is of, is recorded as a stretch from where the writer last knew the file said online. It counts as passed when a batch was sealed online after it, as far as the clocks tell, and never for an end of another boot, which no clock orders with the writer's batches.
- **Why a batch is local-only.** The seal frame of a local-only batch records `why`: `config` when `Options.Online` said no, `noted` when a record came while the writer took the file as local-only, `waiting`, and `late`, the first four late records by kind and time, with how many there were. `Batch.Why` and `Row.Why` carry it. The uploader's `problem.uploader` names the batch the roots stop at, when it was sealed, and these causes, and `pairtime privacy show` adds the same line. A batch an older version sealed keeps the general text, without "a hook call that waited".
- **Counting the drops.** The writer counts dropped records in their stretches, so the count survives restarts (`Status.LateLocal`). The adapter host counts its harness's in `late_local`, which `pairtime doctor`'s adapter line prints, and the agent's log names each drop's kind and time. A drop is not a ledger refusal, and none of its callers reports it as a problem.

## Still open

- Point 5, the adapters' replays of events the ledger already holds, is the second PR.
- A stretch end with no batch sealed after it reaches no seal frame. After a restart the agent's `Seen` gives it, and without that the stretch reads as going on, so it ends at the first read that finds `config.toml` online, later than it did. That is wider, and costs only credit.
- A record of a stretch that comes before any batch was sealed online after it still makes a local-only batch, as point 2 keeps Waiting's rule. After a `config.toml` edit back online, with no sign-in, that batch stops the roots, as any record of that stretch would have (ingest-v1 5.3). After a sign-in it falls in the re-link's gap.
- Stretches are kept for the chain's life, as point 1 says, in seal frames and rows under their own retention, and in the base frame after a sweep. They hold when `config.toml` changed mode, never content.
- Commit records don't upload yet. A git change the writer drops still has its commit records in `commits/`, and they need the same rule once the uploader sends them, as #174 says.
