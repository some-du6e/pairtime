One docs and fixtures PR, which WP-TR1 and WP-TR2 wait for, applies the decided text to DESIGN 7.3, 6.5 and 7.8, SM 1.2, 2.2 rule 8 and 8.7, and `testdata/checks`, and a PR under `oracle-change` then applies it to DESIGN 6.4's corroborated band and the evidence page. Every scenario's `expected.json` names this erratum. The list under each reading names the verdicts that rest on it. A verdict not listed follows from DESIGN's text alone.

## Input

DESIGN 7.3: "Each check reports pass, fail or not computable, with counts. Not computable, such as CC1 on a harness without a log reader, never counts as a failure. Failures change review priority and the band, never the credited number, except CC6 and CC7, which hold minutes." Then the table, one row per check, with what each compares and what it catches. DESIGN 7.7 shows a count as "prompts with keyboard input before them: 212 of 214".

## Expected

Every result in a check fixture follows from DESIGN 7.3, the ledger spec and SM, so that `pkg/check` (WP-TR1, WP-TR2) can be tested against it.

## What DESIGN leaves open, and what the fixtures do

1. **A check with nothing to compare.** A submission with no commit gives CC3 and CC4 nothing to check, and one with no editor act gives CC10 nothing. DESIGN doesn't say whether that is a pass or not computable.
   The fixtures say not computable, with a reason. A pass would put "CC3 passed" on a submission that has no commits at all, which is evidence of nothing. The verdicts that rest on it: CC3, CC4 and CC10 in all thirteen `4.2-*` scenarios, and CC5, CC8 and CC11 in `4.2-cc2-send-keys`, where no prompt opened an anchor.

2. **Who CC2 counts.** CC2 compares "the share of human-origin prompts with OS input in the 10 s before submit and focus on the host window". SM 2.2 rule 6 gives a prompt a human origin only when it had that input and that focus, so read literally the share is always 100%, and DESIGN 7.7's "212 of 214" can't happen. The prompts it can catch, the ones a pty or `tmux send-keys` driver sends, get origin `no_input` under rule 8.
   The fixtures count every prompt that SM 2.2 rules 1 to 5 leave unlabeled: the ones that end up `human_typed`, `human_pasted` or `no_input`. The count is how many had the input and the focus. Every CC2 verdict rests on it.

3. **Shares without a threshold.** DESIGN states CC2 as a share and CC11's injected part as one ("injected share"), and gives no share at which either fails. It doesn't give the number of key-downs that is "too low" for a typed length in CC11 either.
   No verdict rests on these. The CC2 scenarios have all prompts with input or none (`4.2-cc2-send-keys` has 0 of 5). In the CC11 scenarios every prompt has at least as many key-downs as typed characters, 66 for 60, except B2 in `4.2-cc11-pasted`, which has 3 for 60, and no key is injected. Any threshold from a quarter of the typed characters to all of them passes and fails the same scenarios. The generator refuses a CC2 share strictly between none and all, and a CC11 count between a quarter of the credited characters and all of them, so a new scenario can't come to rest on a threshold without someone noticing.

4. **One failing item.** The other checks are stated per item: each hook prompt (CC1), each commit (CC3, CC4), each slot (CC5), each minute (CC6), each batch (CC7), each challenged turn (CC8), each session (CC9), each editor anchor (CC10), each prompt's key count (CC11). DESIGN doesn't say whether one failing item fails the check.
   The fixtures fail a check on one failing item. The "Catches" column needs that reading: a script that invents one prompt, injects four heartbeats or drops one record must be caught. The verdicts that rest on it: CC1 in `4.2-cc1-no-transcript` (1 of 5 prompts), CC5 in `4.2-cc5-second-device` (19 slots credited twice; `slots_credited` counts 75 slot credits, 56 on the Mac and 19 on the laptop), CC6 in `6.3-cc6-moved-minute` (1 of 30 minutes), CC7 in `4.2-cc7-dropped-record` and `4.2-cc7-dropped-batch` (1 of 27 batches), CC8 in `4.2-cc8-false-sketch` and `4.2-cc8-made-up-key` (3 of 5, every turn of the text whose values conflict) and `4.2-cc8-false-class` and `4.2-cc8-inflated-length` (1 of 4), CC9 in `6.3-cc9-moved-turn` (1 of 3 sessions) and `6.3-cc9-orphan-turn` (1 of 4), CC10 in `6.3-cc10-injected-heartbeats` (4 of 14 acts) and `6.3-cc10-wrong-project` (3 of 13) and CC11 in `4.2-cc11-pasted` (1 of 5 prompts).

5. **Some items not computable.** DESIGN's example is a whole check that isn't computable. A submission can mix a harness with a log reader and one without, as 6.3 mixes Claude Code and Codex with Cursor.
   The fixtures compute what they can. A check fails when a computed item fails, passes when it computed at least one item and none failed, and is not computable when it computed none. The items it couldn't compute are counted. The verdicts that rest on it: CC1 passes in all seven `6.3-*` scenarios, with Cursor's two prompts counted as not computable.

6. **Which batches the reveal covers, and which minutes a broken chain holds.** DESIGN 7.5 step 3 has the client reveal "every batch overlapping the re-score range", and CC7 catches "hidden, dropped or reordered records". CC7 says "Affected minutes are held", and DESIGN 6.2 D7 holds minutes "whose chain doesn't verify". DESIGN says neither how CC7 sees a batch the reveal leaves out nor which minutes it then holds.
   The fixtures check the reveal against the server's own rows. Every batch the server stored for the re-score range must be revealed, and each one that isn't counts as `missing_batches` and fails. Without that, a reveal that leaves out batches at either end of the range shows no gap in `b` or `seq` and nothing that fails to reproduce. A batch that is missing or fails holds every minute its records could fall in: from the minute of the last verified record before it to the minute of the first verified record after it, or of the batch's `received_at` when no verified record follows. Its own revealed records can't narrow that, because they are what failed to verify, and a missing batch has none. The verifier checks each batch against the head the server stored before it, so one bad batch doesn't fail every later one. The verdicts that rest on it: `4.2-cc7-dropped-batch` fails CC7, and holds 19:45; `4.2-cc7-dropped-record` holds 19:33 and 19:34, 120 s. Read as the minutes of the failing batch's revealed records, as this erratum first proposed, `4.2-cc7-dropped-record` holds 19:33 alone, a missing or emptied batch holds nothing, and a batch hidden in the middle of the range holds the minutes of the one after it.

7. **The scope of CC5.** "No slot credited twice across devices or projects" could mean the submitted projects or every project of the account.
   The fixtures take every device and every project of the account in the window, which is what "across projects" and SM 3.4's "one human-minute per wall-clock minute per account across all devices" say. The verdict that rests on it: CC5 fails in `4.2-cc5-second-device`, where the second device credits `remind-bot`, a project the submission doesn't include. Read the other way, that scenario passes CC5.

8. **CC1's normalized length and CC3's recorded lines.** CC1 compares "normalized `len_chars`" and doesn't say what is normalized. CC3 compares "lines changed by agent edit tools plus hand-edit deltas in a commit interval". It doesn't say whose records count, whether an editor heartbeat that reports an agent's edit (`edit_source` `agent`, as Cursor's heartbeat in 6.2 does) counts as a hand-edit delta, or where the interval starts when the parent commit is older than the records.
   Every transcript copy has its hook's exact `len_chars`, so no verdict rests on CC1's part. CC3 counts the agent's tool lines and every editor heartbeat except `edit_source` `agent`, since the commit before it in the project, and only from records whose `via` D19 trusts for `logged_s`: `hook`, `log`, `otel`, `editor`, and `bridge` records from heartbeats that passed the peer-process check. Records via `plugin`, `track`, `bridge_unverified` and `editor_unverified` are claims "from any process on the machine" (DESIGN 5.2), and one line in the plugin inbox would otherwise explain any commit. The verdict that rests on it: CC3 fails in `6.2-cc3-plugin-claim`, whose plugin record claims the 300 copied lines; counted, it explains them and CC3 passes. The verdicts are the same with agent-sourced heartbeats counted: 6.2's commit leaves 1 or 0 lines unexplained, and `6.2-cc3-copied-lines` and `6.2-cc3-plugin-claim` leave 300 or 260.

9. **CC6 in the minutes CC7 holds.** A reveal that leaves records out can't be re-scored exactly, so the re-score can differ from the live buckets wherever those records count. DESIGN doesn't say whether that is a CC6 mismatch as well.
   The fixtures compare live and re-scored buckets only in the minutes of W that CC7 doesn't hold. A held minute is held whatever CC6 finds, and comparing it would make every CC7 failure a CC6 failure too, while DESIGN's table keeps them apart: CC6 catches modified clients and scorer bugs, CC7 hidden records. The verdict that rests on it: CC6 passes in `4.2-cc7-dropped-batch`, whose missing batch, A3's stop and the 19:46:00 sample, bears only on 19:45. `4.2-cc7-dropped-record` passes CC6 under either reading.

10. **What CC8 checks against the revealed text.** DESIGN 7.3 and ledger-v1.md 10.8 have CC8 check a challenged prompt's lengths, `low_effort`, `es` and class against its revealed text, and equal `th` and `mh` between revealed prompts with the same normalized text. Neither says which typed length the class is recomputed with, though SM 2.2's class depends on it and the body states it, nor how a conflict between prompts of one text is counted when 10.8 fixes no order for the reveals.
   When nothing was removed, the fixtures require the body's `len_chars` to be the revealed text's length and its `typed_len_est` to be no more than that, and recompute the class with the typed length bounded by the text's. Read from the body's own lengths, the class check passes a client that inflates `len_chars` and `typed_len_est` together, on the hook's record and the log's copy alike, which grows a prompt's typing lead up to 300 s. A conflict counts once in `feature_conflicts` for each normalized text, and every prompt of that text fails, because the server can't tell which values are honest. Counted per later reveal that disagrees with an earlier one, the number depends on the order the reveals are walked in: 1 or 2 in `4.2-cc8-made-up-key`. The verdict that rests on it: `feature_conflicts` is 1 in `4.2-cc8-false-sketch` and `4.2-cc8-made-up-key`, where a count per pair would give 2. CC8's other verdicts follow from 10.8 alone. `4.2-cc8-inflated-length` fails `lengths`, 10.8's rule 1, and its B2 is substantive at 60 typed characters and at 450, so its class passes under either length. `4.2-cc8-false-class` fails `class` with honest lengths. A1, A2 and A3 fail in `4.2-cc8-false-sketch` and `4.2-cc8-made-up-key` because 10.8 fails every turn of a pair that breaks the rule.

11. **Which key-downs CC11 counts.** DESIGN 7.3 compares "key-down counts in the lead window against the prompt's typed length". The typing module counts every key-down on the device, and lead windows of prompts in two sessions can overlap, so read literally the lead window holds key-downs typed into any window and the same key-downs can count toward several prompts.
   The fixtures count only the slots whose focus, the last `os.focus` at or before the slot's end, is the window hosting the prompt's session (or the editor, for an IDE session), and each slot toward the first prompt after it, as SM 3.2 gives a terminal slot to the anchor that comes next. Otherwise 60 key-downs typed into a chat window, or into another session's prompt, during a pasted prompt's 160 s lead would pass it. They count the whole lead window, think part included, and not only the typing part. SM 2.3 puts the typing part last as a rule for crediting it, and someone who types a prompt and then rereads it before sending has typed during the think part. No verdict rests on this: in the 4.2 scenarios focus stays on the terminal hosting the session, and no slot falls in two leads.

## For Karim

Answered on 2026-11-20 by the decision below.

1. Confirm readings 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11, or pick another for each. A different choice changes the verdicts listed under it, in a PR with the `oracle-change` label.
2. Give CC2 and CC11 thresholds for `pkg/check`, which WP-TR1 and WP-TR2 need. A proposal:
   - CC2 fails when at least 2 of the prompts it counts lack the input or the focus, and they are at least a quarter of them. That is SM 8.7's starting point for `no_input_prompts` over a different denominator: the flag counts against all prompts, automated ones included, and CC2 against the prompts rules 1 to 5 leave open. So the two can disagree. With 20 `/loop` prompts, 4 typed ones and 2 sent by tmux, the flag sees 2 of 26 and stays quiet, while CC2 sees 2 of 6 and fails. CC2 over all prompts would agree with the flag on public data, but then scheduled prompts would dilute a pty driver's share; the proposal keeps CC2's own denominator. The deployed flag can still use its private threshold.
   - CC11 fails a prompt whose counted key-downs, injected ones aside, are fewer than a quarter of the characters its typing part credits: `COMPOSE_CPS × min(COMPOSE_MAX_S, typed_len_est / COMPOSE_CPS)`, which is `min(typed_len_est, 450)`. The lead window always has room for that. Its typing part alone lasts `typed_len_est / 1.5` s, up to 300 s, so someone composing at SM's 1.5 characters a second types every credited character in it, and a quarter of that rate, about 5 words a minute, still passes. A paste gives about 3 key-downs whatever its length, so it fails from 13 typed characters up. This replaces the first proposal, at least as many key-downs as typed characters, which fails honest long prompts. A 900-character prompt has a 420 s lead, which holds about 630 key-downs even typed nonstop at SM's 1.5 characters a second, and SM's own model puts the typing in the last 300 s, which holds 450. So every prompt over 450 typed characters fails at SM's own rate. Counting from the session's previous prompt or stop would also always have room, but that window can last hours, and any typing into the same terminal in that time would count.
   - CC11 also fails a prompt with any injected key-down in its lead, because DESIGN 7.12 says injected keys "don't count as typing". A teen who uses a text expander would trip that rule. A share such as 10% would spare them.
3. Say whether CC1 should allow any difference in `len_chars`, for example for line endings a transcript writes differently from the hook. The fixtures don't need it.
4. Say whether a session resumed under another parent fails CC9's rule that "the parent class doesn't change mid-session". Someone who quits Claude Code in Terminal and runs `claude --resume` in tmux records a `session.start` with `start_kind` `resume` and parent `multiplexer` under the same session key. The fixtures don't need the answer: the second `session.start` in `4.2-cc9-parent-class` is a `startup`, a second process claiming a live session's key, which no harness records.

## Decision

Decided 2026-11-20 by the orchestrator for Karim. DESIGN 7.3 states each rule, and `testdata/checks` follows it.

1. Readings 1 to 11 stand, with reading 10 as the CC8 decision of issue #15 rewrote it. Reading 6 gains two rules that no verdict rests on. A missing `seq` counts against the batch whose stored `n` covers it, so the batch after it isn't failed for it. A batch that is missing or fails also holds every other minute whose re-score comes out differently without its revealed records. `4.2-cc7-dropped-record` still holds 19:33 and 19:34, 120 s, and `4.2-cc7-dropped-batch` holds 19:45.

2. CC2 fails when at least 2 of the prompts it counts, and at least a quarter of them, lack the input or the focus. SM 2.2 rule 8 and 8.7 now count `no_input_prompts` over the same prompts, so `/loop` prompts no longer dilute the flag. CC11 fails a prompt whose hardware key-downs are fewer than a quarter of `min(typed length, 450)`, or fewer than its injected key-downs. One injected key no longer fails a prompt, so a text expander fails it only when its keys outnumber the typed ones. Dictation and on-screen keyboards still fail CC11, as a paste does. That changes review priority and the band, never the credit (DESIGN 7.3, 7.11).

3. CC1 allows no difference in `len_chars`. SM 1.2 counts it in code points of the prompt as the harness delivered it, pasted blocks expanded, with no line-break normalization, the same count CC8's lengths rule uses. WP-AD3's capture test checks that each transcript gives its hook's `len_chars`.

4. A session's parent class may change at a `session.start` with `start_kind` `resume`, and only among `terminal_app`, `ide`, `multiplexer` and `remote`. So `claude --resume` in tmux after quitting in Terminal passes CC9, even when the terminal closed without a `SessionEnd`, and no resume can drop a headless parent without CC9 failing. `4.2-cc9-parent-class` still fails, because its second start is a `startup`.

5. DESIGN 6.4's corroborated band now asks that a row fail none of CC6, CC7 and CC9, so a hand-coding row, where CC9 has no session and isn't computable, can reach it.
