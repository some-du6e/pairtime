# Erratum: SM 6.1's 173 s and SM 2.1's unread prose words

Date 2026-11-20. Raised by the WP-SC1 review fix-up (#59). Status: decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator for Karim (Decision, below): option 2, measured when the tail ends, plus the prose that goes into the bucket later in the run, with side doors left out. SM 6.1's 173 s and SM 2.1's 53 + 120 = 173 s stand, and SM 2.1's definition changes. No fixture row depends on the answer: 6.1's drink ends with a click in the editor, which the return rule doesn't credit either way. SM 2.1 and 6.1 take the text, with `pkg/score`'s code, under `oracle-change` in WP-HL1's one `pkg/score` PR for the errata decided on 2026-11-23. That PR takes `pkg/score/VERSION`'s next MINOR, and until it lands `pkg/score` follows SM 2.1's definition as it stands.

## Input

- SM 2.1 defines the reading part of `RETURN_CREDIT`: "`unread_prose_words` counts only the prose of agent output that arrived since the person last had the session in focus with input." It closes with an example: "a 6-minute silence after a 160-word answer, followed straight away by a typed prompt, earns at most 53 + 120 = 173 s".
- SM 6.1, cycle 6, which `testdata/timelines/6.1` records. The agent stops at 15:06:30 with its 160-word summary (an `agent.output` of 160 words and the `agent.stop`, both at 15:06:30). Rowan's last input is at 15:06:40, in the iTerm window that hosts the session: the samples at 15:06:45 and 15:07:00 report idle times of 5 s and 20 s. The 30 s tail runs to 15:07:10. SM says: "Had he sat down and typed a prompt straight away, the return rule would have credited at most 53 + 120 = 173 s of the silence (2.1)."

## Expected

173 s: 53 s of reading for the 160-word summary (60 × 160 / 180), placed after the tail as `READ~r`, and the prompt's 120 s think lead as `P~r`.

## What the rules give

The summary arrived at 15:06:30, and Rowan last had the session in focus with input at 15:06:40, ten seconds later. By SM 2.1's definition none of it is unread, so `unread_prose_words` is 0 and the credit is the think lead alone: 120 s, with no `READ~r`.

No reading of "since the person last had the session in focus with input" gives 160 words here. Measured from the end of that stretch of focus it gives 0. Measured from its start, when he switched to iTerm to type the cycle's prompt, it counts all of the turn's prose since 15:04, 45 + 45 + 160 = 250 words. SM 2.1's own example gives 173 s when the answer arrives after the last touch, which is the timing `TestReturnRule`'s first case uses. Cycle 6 has the answer arrive ten seconds before it.

## What the scorer does

It follows SM 2.1's definition until the PR that applies this decision. `TestReturnRule` has a case with 6.1's timing, an answer ten seconds before the last touch and then a typed prompt after six minutes, which credits 120 s and names this erratum. With that PR it follows the decided rule. When the credit pass reaches the first second at or after a returned run's tail end, it counts the hooked and logged prose still in the counted sessions' buckets, adds the prose that goes into them later in the run, and sets the run's `UnreadWords`, `ReadUntil` and `CreditMS` from that. `TestReturnRule` then has 6.1's bucket, two 30-line edits and then the summary ten seconds before the last touch, and credits 53 + 120 = 173 s. With the summary alone in the bucket, as the old 6.1-timing case had it, the tail reads 40 s of it and the rule credits 13 + 120 = 133 s.

## For Karim

Pick one:

1. Keep SM 2.1's definition, and correct SM 6.1's sentence to 120 s. Ten seconds after the summary landed, Rowan could have read 30 of its words; the definition treats a touch in the window as having seen the output.
2. Count the prose still unread in the session's reading bucket when the silence starts (SM 2.4). Draining takes the oldest entry first, so at 15:06:40 the summary, the newest entry, is still all there, and 6.1 gives 173 s. This reads "unread" as "not yet drained", which is what the bucket already tracks.
3. Count the prose since the session's last human anchor, the prompt that started the turn. 6.1 then gives 173 s too, but output that a person demonstrably scrolled through before leaving counts again.

Option 2 or 3 changes SM 2.1's sentence. Whichever is chosen, `TestReturnRule`'s 6.1 case changes with it.

## Decision

Decided 2026-11-23 by the orchestrator, under Karim's delegation: option 2, measured when the tail ends instead of when the silence starts. `unread_prose_words` counts the prose still in the reading buckets (SM 2.4) of the sessions the focused window hosts, or of all the project's sessions when it hosts none, when the tail ends, plus the prose that goes into them later in the run. Only prose that the harness's own hooks or logs report counts (DESIGN 5.2). Each second credited as reading drains a bucket oldest entry first, so what the buckets still hold is reading nobody has been credited for. Measuring at the tail's end keeps the tail's own reading from counting twice. Counting the prose that arrives later keeps what the old rule credited for an answer that lands just after the tail. The reading part still sits right after the tail and drains only prose already in the bucket, so `RETURN_CREDIT` stays a ceiling. A returned run is final from its tail's end, since its reading part depends on what the tail's seconds drained. For a teen: if you go quiet for up to ten minutes and come back with a real prompt, edit, command or commit, pairtime credits reading for the answer text it hasn't credited you for yet, plus the thinking time before that act, up to two minutes.

The check. In cycle 6 the bucket at 15:07:10 holds the first edit's 135 s, the second edit's 225 s and the summary's 53⅓ s. The 40 s of reading from the stop to the end of the tail came out of the first edit, so all 160 words are unread, the reading part is 53 s, and the return rule would have credited at most 53 + 120 = 173 s. SM's numbers stand.

Why the other options lost. Option 1: ten seconds after a 160-word summary, Rowan could have read about 30 of its words, and a rule that treats a touch as having seen the output gives nothing to the reader who scrolls once and then reads silently, the person the return rule exists for. Option 3: its formula counts 250 words, 83 + 120 = 203 s, prose he had already read included, and it reaches 173 s only because the bucket has nothing but the summary's prose left to drain. The session's last human anchor before the silence is also the 15:05:25 permission answer, not the 15:04:00 prompt, so option 3 as worded names two different points.

What it costs. No parameter value changes, and no fixture row moves. Of the timelines' four silences of 120 to 700 s, 6.1's drink ends in `NAV`, 6.4's setup silence has an empty bucket until after its run, `relayed/t3-here`'s act comes more than 600 s after its silence starts, and `relayed/t3-here-agent`'s silence has no act after it.
