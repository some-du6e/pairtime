# Erratum: SM 6.1's 173 s and SM 2.1's unread prose words

Date 2026-11-20. Raised by the WP-SC1 review fix-up (#59). Status: waiting for Karim. `pkg/score` follows SM 2.1's definition, and no fixture row depends on the answer: 6.1's drink ends with a click in the editor, which the return rule doesn't credit either way.

## Input

- SM 2.1 defines the reading part of `RETURN_CREDIT`: "`unread_prose_words` counts only the prose of agent output that arrived since the person last had the session in focus with input." It closes with an example: "a 6-minute silence after a 160-word answer, followed straight away by a typed prompt, earns at most 53 + 120 = 173 s".
- SM 6.1, cycle 6, which `testdata/timelines/6.1` records. The agent stops at 15:06:30 with its 160-word summary (an `agent.output` of 160 words and the `agent.stop`, both at 15:06:30). Rowan's last input is at 15:06:40, in the iTerm window that hosts the session: the samples at 15:06:45 and 15:07:00 report idle times of 5 s and 20 s. The 30 s tail runs to 15:07:10. SM says: "Had he sat down and typed a prompt straight away, the return rule would have credited at most 53 + 120 = 173 s of the silence (2.1)."

## Expected

173 s: 53 s of reading for the 160-word summary (60 × 160 / 180), placed after the tail as `READ~r`, and the prompt's 120 s think lead as `P~r`.

## What the rules give

The summary arrived at 15:06:30, and Rowan last had the session in focus with input at 15:06:40, ten seconds later. By SM 2.1's definition none of it is unread, so `unread_prose_words` is 0 and the credit is the think lead alone: 120 s, with no `READ~r`.

No reading of "since the person last had the session in focus with input" gives 160 words here. Measured from the end of that stretch of focus it gives 0. Measured from its start, when he switched to iTerm to type the cycle's prompt, it counts all of the turn's prose since 15:04, 45 + 45 + 160 = 250 words. SM 2.1's own example gives 173 s when the answer arrives after the last touch, which is the timing `TestReturnRule`'s first case uses. Cycle 6 has the answer arrive ten seconds before it.

## What the scorer does

It follows SM 2.1's definition. `TestReturnRule` has a case with 6.1's timing, an answer ten seconds before the last touch and then a typed prompt after six minutes, which credits 120 s and names this erratum. The code isn't fitted to 6.1's number.

## For Karim

Pick one:

1. Keep SM 2.1's definition, and correct SM 6.1's sentence to 120 s. Ten seconds after the summary landed, Rowan could have read 30 of its words; the definition treats a touch in the window as having seen the output.
